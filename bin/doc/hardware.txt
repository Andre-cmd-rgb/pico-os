This board
==========

Freenove ESP32-S3 Display (FNK0104A/B): a 240 MHz
dual core with 8 MB of PSRAM and 16 MB of flash, a
2.8" 320x240 screen, a card slot, a codec and a
battery charger.

WHAT IS WIRED TO WHAT

  screen    SPI2: CS 10, MOSI 11, SCK 12, DC 46,
            backlight 45, reset follows the board's
  card      SDMMC 4-bit: CLK 38 CMD 40 D0 39 D1 41
            D2 48 D3 47
  sound     ES8311 at 0x18 on I2C SDA 16 / SCL 15;
            I2S MCLK 4 BCLK 5 WS 7 DOUT 8 DIN 6;
            amplifier enable on 1 (low = playing)
  battery   sense on GPIO9 through a divider of one
            half
  LED       WS2812 on 42        button  BOOT on 0
  free      GPIO 2, 3, 14, 21 on the header, and 17
            and 18 on the version without touch
  header    UART TX 43 / RX 44 for a SIM module

CHECKING IT

  dmesg               what each driver found at boot
  i2cdetect           what answers on the I2C bus
                      (in a development build)
  lcdtest             colour bars
  backlight 0-100     dim the screen
  beep / rec / play   the sound path
  battery             the cell
  df / ls /mnt/sd     the card

THINGS WORTH KNOWING

  The battery sense hangs off the charger, so with
  USB plugged in and no cell it reads about 5 V. The
  system treats anything above 4.3 V as "no battery
  fitted" and leaves the status bar's corner empty.
  There is no current sensor: the level, cycles and
  health are worked out from the voltage and what
  the board is doing (`battery -v`). After fitting a
  new cell, `battery -c MAH` with its capacity.

  The CardKB wakes the board from `suspend` (any key)
  and `poweroff` (Enter): the low-power core asks it
  for a key every 100 ms on its own pins, so a board
  in a case needs neither button.

  The panel is driven at 40 MHz, faster than its
  datasheet allows but reliable here. At 80 it now
  and then writes a pixel twice in a long run, and
  the rest of that run lands one place on: `lcdtest
  verify` reads the panel back to check. While a clip
  plays it refreshes at 48 Hz instead of 71, so that
  a frame, 31 ms on the bus, fits between two.

  A CardKB on the I2C header shares those wires with
  the codec, so power it from 3V3, never 5 V.

  Hosting USB (a stick or a keyboard) means the board
  supplies 5 V to it, which this socket cannot do: it
  needs a powered hub, and the console has to move to
  the network first.

  A SIM module needs its own supply. They pull two
  amps for a moment when they talk to a tower, which
  browns out anything sharing their power.
